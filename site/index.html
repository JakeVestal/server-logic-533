Telling Sanji What to Trade FINTECH 533

DRAFT   |   the file is the order   |   Sanji does not fill these yet

01 // What you are writing

Your nightly script does not talk to a broker. It writes mkt/open_orders.json. Sanji reads that file after the session, looks up the prices he stored, and decides what filled. The JSON is the order.

Sanji is the program that fetches prices and resolves orders. He runs on Luffy, the course machine. The prices he used are kept in Robin, the course database. You do not write to Robin, and you do not edit your positions file. You read positions and you write orders.

Sanji does not resolve these orders yet. Write them in this shape now, so the file is already right when he does.

02 // Two files

Both live in your repo under mkt/.

positions.json Sanji writes this. It is the book as of the end of the most recent session. Your script reads it. Your script does not change it.
open_orders.json You write this. It is a JSON array of orders for a coming session. One object per order. An empty book of orders is [].

A session is one trading day. The date on the order is that day, written YYYY-MM-DD.

03 // When your script runs

The market is closed. You can see yesterday's book and yesterday's prices. You cannot see the open you are about to trade against. That is the point of the strike and price language below: you name the contract relative to a print that has not happened yet.

Sanji resolves session S, and updates positions.json to the close of S, before your script plans session S+1. If you run first, you are planning off a stale book.

04 // One order

An unknown field, an unknown token, a missing required field, or a quantity below 1 is rejected. Sanji does not guess.

id string, required Yours. Unique in the repo. Sanji fills an id once. A second run the same night does not fill it again.
for YYYY-MM-DD The session the order belongs to. Not "next". A late run must not fill Monday's order on Tuesday.
order_type mkt or lmt Market, or limit. A market order fills at the open of the resolved contract. A limit order needs lmt_prc.
action BUY SEL SHT BTC Buy to open, sell to close, sell to open, buy to close. Quantity is always a positive integer. One order cannot flip a long into a short.
qty integer ≥ 1 Option contracts, or shares when contract is underlying.
underlying conid Interactive Brokers' integer id for the stock. Not a ticker. Copy it from price_history.json.
contract put call underlying Required. Blank is rejected.
expiry token, below Required for a put or a call. Omit it for the underlying.
strike token, below Required for a put or a call. Omit it for the underlying.
ref open or prev_close Which print the strike tokens use. Default is open. This is the slot a clock time will occupy later.
lmt_prc token, below Required when order_type is lmt. Forbidden when it is mkt.

05 // A wheel, written as an order

The book is only cash. A wheel writes a near-the-money put. "Write" is SHT, sell to open. The strike is not a number yet, because it depends on tomorrow's open. One strike out of the money, at the midpoint of that put's bid and ask, is a limit order. It is not a market order. 

[
  {
    "id": "tsla-csp-2026-11-05",
    "for": "2026-11-05",
    "order_type": "lmt",
    "action": "SHT",
    "qty": 1,
    "underlying": 76792991,
    "contract": "put",
    "expiry": "5DTE+",
    "strike": "otm:1",
    "ref": "open",
    "lmt_prc": "mid"
  }
]

76792991 is Tesla. Take the conid from your own price_history.json rather than typing a ticker. 5DTE+ is the listed expiry nearest to five calendar days out, on or after that fifth day. otm:1 is one listed strike below the strike closest to the open. mid is the midpoint of that put at the open.

The same contract as a market order drops lmt_prc and sets order_type to mkt. Sanji fills it at the put's open. A 30-delta version changes only the strike, to delta:30. Thirty means absolute delta 0.30. You do not write the minus sign.

Buying the shares outright, if that is what the book calls for:

{
  "id": "tsla-shares-2026-11-05",
  "for": "2026-11-05",
  "order_type": "mkt",
  "action": "BUY",
  "qty": 100,
  "underlying": 76792991,
  "contract": "underlying",
  "ref": "open"
}

06 // Actions

BUY Open or add to a long. Shares, or a put or call you are buying.
SEL Close or reduce a long. Selling shares you were assigned is SEL. Selling a put you own is SEL.
SHT Open or add to a short. Writing a put or a covered call is SHT.
BTC Close or reduce a short. Buying the put back is BTC.

SEL with no long to sell is rejected. BTC with no short to cover is rejected. Sanji does not check buying power. A cash-secured put larger than the cash still fills. That mistake is yours.

07 // Expiry

Days are calendar days, counted from for, not from the night you wrote the file. Sunday night, ordering for Monday, five days is counted from Monday.

5DTE+ Listed expiry nearest to 5 days out, with at least 5 days left.
5DTE- Nearest listed expiry with 5 days or fewer.
5DTE That calendar date only. No listed expiry that day means no fill.
2026-11-20 That listed date.
W+ Next weekly.
M+ Next monthly. The third Friday.

08 // Strike

Anything that is not already a number snaps to a listed strike. ATM is the listed strike closest to ref. A tie goes to the lower strike. otm and itm count steps from that ATM. A put's out-of-the-money direction is down. A call's is up.

250 That strike. No chain needed to know which contract you mean.
atm Closest listed strike to ref.
otm:1 One listed strike out of the money from ATM. itm:1 is one strike in.
pct:-5 ref times 0.95, then the nearest listed strike. A tie goes further out of the money.
delta:30 Nearest listed strike to absolute delta 0.30. A tie goes further out of the money.
premium:2 Nearest strike whose midpoint is $2. Same tie rule.
vega:0.15 Reserved. Same field, not a new one. Rejected until it is turned on.

A bare number needs no option chain. otm, itm, atm, pct, W+, and the DTE forms need the list of strikes and expiries. delta, premium, and vega need a chain snapshot taken at ref. Until Robin has that snapshot, Sanji rejects those tokens. He will not invent a delta from the stock's bar.

09 // Limit price

3.50 That premium. For the underlying, that share price.
mid Midpoint of the resolved contract at ref. bid and ask are the quote itself.
mid-0.05 That quote, plus a dollar offset. bid+0.10 and ask-0.05 are the same idea.

mid, bid, and ask need the quote at ref. A numeric limit does not.

10 // What Sanji does with the file

Once the session's prices are in Robin, Sanji takes every order whose for is that session and whose id is not already filled.

1 Resolve the expiry, then the strike. If the print or the chain he needs is missing, the order is unresolved. It is not tried again on a later day.
2 A market order fills at the resolved contract's open.
3 A limit buy or cover fills at the open if the open is already through the limit, otherwise at the limit if the day's low trades through it. A limit sell or short is the mirror image, using the high. If the bar never trades through, the order is unfilled.
4 Fills are all or nothing. There are no partials.

He appends the result to mkt/fills.json and rewrites positions.json so that it is the book at the end of that session. You may leave old orders in open_orders.json. An id already in the fills file will not fill again.

filled   unfilled   unresolved   rejected

filled and unfilled are outcomes of a contract he could name. unresolved means he could not name the contract from the data he had. rejected means the order itself was not legal.

11 // The book after the close

Assignment is not an order you write. After fills, Sanji applies it from the underlying close. A short put with the close below the strike becomes long 100 shares per contract, and the put is gone. A short call with the close above the strike is gone, and if you are long the shares, those shares are called away. Cash moves by the strike times the multiplier.

Without that, a wheel that was short a put cannot wake up long the stock and write the call. You still do not edit positions.json. You read the book Sanji wrote and, if the book is now long shares, your next order is the covered call.

12 // Not in this version

Multi-leg tickets, partial fills, stop orders, and any reference time other than prev_close and open. A spread, when it comes, will be several of these orders sharing an id, not a new shape.

vega is already a legal-looking strike token so that the field does not have to change. Sanji rejects it until it is defined. Do not invent tokens. An unrecognized token is rejected, not a quiet no-op.
